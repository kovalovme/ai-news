## SambaNova Closes $1B Series F at $11B Valuation <a id="sambanova-1b-series-f"></a>

**Source:** [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/08/sambanova-ai-chip-funding-valuation.html) · [General Atlantic](https://www.generalatlantic.com/media-article/sambanova-completes-first-close-of-1-billion-financing-at-11-billion-valuation/) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-08/ai-chip-startup-sambanova-raises-funds-at-11-billion-valuation) · **Type:** funding · **Time (UTC):** July 8

SambaNova Systems completed the first close of a $1 billion Series F financing, valuing the company at $11 billion — more than double its 2021 valuation of $5 billion. The round was led by General Atlantic and included Seligman Ventures, T. Rowe Price Associates, Capital Group, BlackRock, Intel Capital, and the Qatar Investment Authority. JPMorgan Chase was announced simultaneously as SambaNova's flagship inference infrastructure partner, deploying SN40 and SN50 systems for production AI workloads.

Capital will fund expanded computing capacity, global deployments, and continued development of the company's chip, systems, and software stack. Founded in 2017 in Palo Alto, SambaNova had faced fundraising headwinds and acquisition rumors in prior years; this round signals renewed investor confidence in non-Nvidia inference at scale.

**Why it matters:** The JPMorgan deployment is the most credible public validation of SambaNova's production readiness to date. For engineers evaluating inference infrastructure, it puts SambaNova on a short-list alongside Cerebras and Groq as serious alternatives to Nvidia-based deployments, especially for large-scale dedicated inference.

## White House DoC Clears GPT-5.6 for Public Release <a id="white-house-gpt56-clearance"></a>

**Source:** [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2210308/openai-rolls-out-gpt5-6-july-9/) · [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/openai-to-release-gpt-56-sol-terra-and-luna-on-july-9/) · **Type:** policy · **Time (UTC):** July 9

The Department of Commerce's Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) cleared GPT-5.6 for public release on July 9 following additional testing and meetings with OpenAI. The model had been withheld from general availability since late June at the administration's request. The clearance is the first visible output of the EO signed June 2 ("Promoting Advanced AI Innovation and Security"), which established a 30-day pre-release review window for frontier models. The review is explicitly advisory and non-blocking; in this case it appears to have completed within roughly two weeks.

**Why it matters:** This is the first confirmed instance of the voluntary pre-release review process working end-to-end. Developers building on frontier API access should expect similar delays — two to four weeks — to become a structural feature of new frontier releases from US labs.

## Great American Artificial Intelligence Act — Bipartisan Draft Published <a id="gaaia-bipartisan-draft"></a>

**Source:** [Mintz Washington AI Report — July 2026](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/54941/2026-07-08-ai-washington-report-july-2026-edition) · **Type:** policy · **Time (UTC):** July 8

The July 8 edition of Mintz's Washington AI Report highlighted the bipartisan 269-page discussion draft of the Great American Artificial Intelligence Act (GAAIA), released June 4 by Reps. Jay Obernolte (R-CA) and Lori Trahan (D-MA). Key provisions:

- **Frontier model disclosure**: Developers must report model capabilities and obtain audits from designated Independent Verification Organizations (IVOs).
- **State law preemption**: A three-year moratorium on state laws specifically regulating the *development* of AI models; does not cover post-deployment activities or general-purpose privacy and consumer-protection laws.
- **Agency responsibilities**: NIST and NSF are designated as central technical standards bodies.

The House Science, Space, and Technology Committee marked up 10 related AI bills on June 25, all passing with strong bipartisan support.

**Why it matters:** GAAIA is the most detailed comprehensive federal AI governance proposal to reach committee markup. The three-year preemption clause is significant for developers managing state compliance; it would temporarily consolidate regulatory surface at the federal level for model development while leaving product and deployment liability largely at the state layer.
