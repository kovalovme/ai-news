## FTC Proposes AI Accuracy Policy Statement; Comment Period Closes July 31 <a id="ftc-ai-accuracy"></a>

**Source:** [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/07/ftc-seeks-public-comment-policy-statement-addressing-ai-accuracy) · **Type:** policy · **Time (UTC):** —

The Federal Trade Commission issued a proposed policy statement asserting that AI companies that manipulate model outputs "contrary to reasonable consumer expectations for objectivity and accuracy" could constitute deceptive conduct under Section 5 of the FTC Act. The statement was driven in part by a December 2025 executive order directing the FTC to assess state AI output-modification laws; the proposal suggests Colorado's Artificial Intelligence Act may be "impliedly preempted" where it compels changes to AI outputs. A public comment window runs through July 31, 2026 (Docket FTC-2026-0727 on Regulations.gov). The commission voted 2-0 on the proposal.

**Why it matters:** The statement creates the first federal legal framing under which AI labs could face consumer-protection liability for output manipulation, though it stops well short of a rulemaking. The preemption argument is significant: if adopted, it would constrain state-level AI output rules in favor of federal oversight, a major jurisdictional win for AI companies seeking a single national compliance standard. For engineers, the concrete risk is that products that visibly suppress or skew outputs (content filtering, politically sensitive topics) may draw regulatory scrutiny if those choices aren't disclosed.
