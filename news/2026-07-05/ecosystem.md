## Amazon Mechanical Turk closes to new customers July 30 <a id="mechanical-turk-shutdown"></a>

**Source:** [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/off-prem/2026/07/03/amazons-mechanical-turk-to-stop-accepting-new-customers-and-not-even-ai-can-close-it/) · [Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/amazon-winds-down-mechanical-turk-closing-the-2005-microtask-crowdsourcing-marketplace-to-new-customers-on-july-30-2026/) · **Type:** update · **Time (UTC):** July 3, 2026

Amazon placed Mechanical Turk in maintenance mode and announced it will stop accepting new customers on July 30, 2026, effectively winding down the platform. Launched in November 2005, Mechanical Turk was the dominant marketplace for human microtasks — data labeling, content moderation, survey work, image annotation — that fueled over a decade of machine learning training pipelines. Amazon has not stated a final sunset date for existing customers. The company cited SageMaker GroundTruth as its current alternative for data labeling, a service that now uses AI models to auto-label and reduce human review volume.

**Why it matters:** Mechanical Turk's wind-down is a direct measure of how thoroughly AI has replaced human labor in the data annotation pipeline. Research computing communities that depended on MTurk for human-participant studies will need to find alternatives; the platform's declining viability has been visible for two years, but the formal maintenance-mode designation makes it official.

## EU AI Act Digital Omnibus: high-risk compliance deferred to December 2027 <a id="eu-ai-act-omnibus"></a>

**Source:** [EU Council press release](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/05/07/artificial-intelligence-council-and-parliament-agree-to-simplify-and-streamline-rules/) · [Global Policy Watch](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/06/eu-ai-act-update-timeline-relief-targeted-simplification-and-new-prohibitions-2/) · [DLA Piper GENIE](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/The-Digital-AI-Omnibus-Proposed-deferral-of-high-risk-AI-obligations-under-the-AI-Act) · **Type:** policy · **Time (UTC):** June 29, 2026 (final Council approval)

The EU Council gave its final approval on June 29 to the "Digital AI Omnibus," the first set of formal amendments to the EU AI Act since its 2024 adoption. The legislation is now awaiting official journal publication (entering into force three days after). The principal change for practitioners: high-risk AI systems classified under Annex III (use-based risk, e.g. recruitment, credit scoring, biometric categorization) now have until December 2, 2027, to comply — 16 months later than the August 2, 2026 deadline under the original Act. Annex I systems (product-integrated risk, e.g. medical devices, lifts) are deferred from August 2027 to August 2028. Two new prohibitions enter force in December 2026: AI-generated non-consensual intimate imagery and CSAM generation.

**Why it matters:** The 16-month deferral is practically significant for any enterprise that was budgeting for August 2026 compliance across Annex III use cases. The extension reflects the difficulty of building conformity assessment processes before the underlying EU standards (from CEN-CENELEC) are ready. US companies selling high-risk AI into the EU market gain more preparation time, but the compliance machinery still needs to start now.

| Obligation type | Original deadline | New deadline | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annex III (use-based HRAIS) | Aug 2, 2026 | Dec 2, 2027 | +16 months |
| Annex I (product-integrated HRAIS) | Aug 2, 2027 | Aug 2, 2028 | +12 months |
| New prohibitions (NCII / CSAM) | — | Dec 2, 2026 | new |

## OpenAI Stargate UK: Guardian investigation finds Cobalt site was never visited <a id="stargate-uk-cobalt"></a>

**Source:** [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-apparently-never-visited-the-site-of-its-flagship-uk-ai-project) · [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/09/openai-halts-uk-stargate-project.html) · **Type:** controversy · **Time (UTC):** July 4, 2026

A Guardian investigation published July 4 found that OpenAI never physically visited Cobalt Park — the site in the North East designated as the UK's flagship Stargate AI data center — before the partnership was announced in September 2025 during Donald Trump's UK state visit. On-site reporting found the location to be a scaffolding yard with no evidence of construction activity. OpenAI already paused Stargate UK in April 2026, citing UK industrial electricity costs roughly four times higher than the US and unresolved AI/copyright regulatory questions. The revelation raises broader questions about how much of the UK's publicized AI infrastructure investment pipeline is based on signed agreements versus press-release announcements.

**Why it matters:** For engineers evaluating UK or European AI infrastructure options, the Stargate UK episode is a caution against treating government-endorsed AI investment figures as supply-side capacity estimates. The gap between announcement and build-out is apparently larger than presented.

## White House voluntary frontier model framework: announcement expected July 7 <a id="white-house-voluntary-standards"></a>

**Source:** [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/white-house-nears-voluntary-frontier-model-deal-with-top-ai-labs) · [White House EO](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/promoting-advanced-artificial-intelligence-innovation-and-security/) · **Type:** policy · **Time (UTC):** —

The White House is in final negotiations with OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google on a voluntary framework that would establish benchmarks for frontier model releases, pre-release government access windows, and rules for who can access the next tier of models domestically and abroad. An announcement is expected as early as the week of July 7. The framework builds on the June 2 executive order, which created voluntary pre-release review slots (up to 30 days) for models with advanced cybersecurity capabilities, without creating mandatory licensing or preclearance requirements.

**Why it matters:** If finalized, the voluntary framework effectively sets a policy template for frontier model release cadences that could outlast any one administration and inform legislation. For labs outside the US, it also establishes access criteria that could be the precursor to formal export controls or interoperability requirements.
