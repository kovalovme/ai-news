## Anthropic AI for Science: rare disease research grant call <a id="anthropic-rare-disease-grants"></a>

**Source:** [anthropic.com/news/rare-disease-research-grants](https://www.anthropic.com/news/rare-disease-research-grants) · **Type:** grant program · **Time (UTC):** Jul 20 ~06:00

Anthropic opened a focused call for rare genetic disease research projects within its broader AI for Science program. Accepted applicants receive up to $50,000 in Claude API credits over six months, plus research support from the Anthropic team.

**Application deadline:** August 2, 2026 at 23:59 PST.

Examples of current AI for Science grantees in adjacent work: Every Cure (drug repurposing identification), Centre for Population Genomics (variant classification system), Violet Research Institute (FDA guideline navigation for ultra-rare diseases).

**Why it matters:** At current Sonnet 5 pricing ($3/MTok in, $15/MTok out), $50,000 in credits represents on the order of 3–5 million tokens of compute — enough to run large-scale literature synthesis and variant analysis workflows that were previously cost-prohibitive for academic labs. Rare disease work is specifically a domain where data scarcity makes AI augmentation high-leverage.

## Bloomy (YC S26): AI mastery-learning platform for K-12 launches on HN <a id="bloomy-yc-s26"></a>

**Source:** [Launch HN thread — 85 pts](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=...) · **Type:** product launch · **Time (UTC):** Jul 21

Bloomy, a YC Summer 2026 company, launched on HN today with 85 points. The product applies mastery-learning pedagogy — where students must demonstrate competency before advancing — using an AI tutor that adapts question sequencing and explanations in real time. Targets K-12 classrooms and individual learners.

No pricing or institutional contract details were disclosed in the launch post.

**Why it matters:** Mastery learning has decades of evidence behind it as more effective than grade-based progression, but has historically been difficult to implement at scale without high teacher-to-student ratios. AI tutors that adapt in real time are a natural fit for this pedagogy, and YC backing signals institutional interest in edtech AI beyond generic homework help.
